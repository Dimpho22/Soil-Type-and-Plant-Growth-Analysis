Soil Type and Plant Growth Analysis

Project Overview

This project investigates the relationship between soil composition and plant growth at Tswaing Crater, South Africa. It examines how the proportions of sand and clay in the soil relate to vegetation characteristics across three study sites located at the top, middle, and bottom of a catena (a sequence of soils along a slope).

The analysis focuses on soil composition and four plant growth indicators: herbaceous biomass, woody biomass, mean tree height, and maximum tree height.

Objectives

* Compare sand and clay percentages across the three study sites.
* Examine how plant biomass varies between sites.
* Investigate the relationship between soil composition and tree height.
* Present the results using graphs to identify patterns in soil and vegetation characteristics.

Study Area

The study was conducted at Tswaing Crater, approximately 50 km north of Pretoria, South Africa. Soil and vegetation data were collected from three sites positioned along a catena.

Data Analysed

The project compares the following variables:

Category	Variables
Soil composition	Sand percentage and clay percentage
Herbaceous vegetation	Mean herbaceous biomass (tons/ha)
Woody vegetation	Mean woody biomass (kg)
Tree growth	Mean tree height (m) and maximum tree height (m)
Study locations	Bottom, middle, and top of the catena

Key Findings

* Soil composition: Sand content increased from 36.0% at the bottom site to 69.4% at the top, while clay content decreased from 31.6% to 12.9%.
* Herbaceous biomass: Mean biomass was highest at the bottom site (2.54 tons/ha) and lowest at the top site (1.36 tons/ha).
* Woody biomass: Mean woody biomass increased from 0.05 kg at the bottom to 0.07 kg at the top.
* Mean tree height: Trees were tallest on average at the middle site, at 1.92 m.
* Maximum tree height: The highest recorded site-level mean maximum tree height was 3.47 m at the top site.

These results illustrate differences in soil composition and vegetation characteristics across the study sites.

Conclusion

This project explores how soil composition and plant growth characteristics vary across a landscape. By comparing three sites at Tswaing Crater, it demonstrates how ecological data can be used to investigate relationships between soil properties and vegetation patterns.

The project connects biological science, environmental analysis, and data visualisation to support the interpretation of ecological patterns.
